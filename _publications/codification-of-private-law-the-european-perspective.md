> **Abstract:**
> This chapter reassesses the case for a shared framework of private law in the European Union after the abandonment of the proposed Common European Sales Law. It identifies economic integration and clearer access to EU law as the strongest reasons for further codification. Examples from legislation and judicial decisions show how isolated European rules can be difficult to interpret and apply without a common foundation of private law. The analysis also considers the role of general rules in organising legal reasoning and shaping legal education. It argues for a legislative framework of basic private law rules that could connect fragmented EU measures and provide a shared point of reference for legal practice and teaching across Europe.

**Editorial contribution:** Jannis Gries finalised the posthumous manuscript for publication without changes or additions to its content.
